Thousands of starlings, each following three simple rules. Move through the sky to scare the flock like a falcon — or tune the rules and watch the pattern change.

How it works

Cohesion steers each bird toward the centre of its neighbours. Alignment matches their heading. Separation avoids collisions.

Real starlings track about 7 nearest neighbours regardless of distance (Ballerini et al., 2008) — a topological rule that keeps the flock cohesive at any density. Try changing "Neighbours". A soft roost pull keeps the flock drifting through the view, and your pointer (or finger) acts as a predator.
